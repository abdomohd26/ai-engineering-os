# Engineering Principles

---

## 1. Delivery & Execution

### Principle 1 — Delivery First

Delivery is the first priority.

Long-term learning must never reduce delivery quality, reliability, or deadlines.

### Principle 12 — Ship, Then Improve

Ship, then improve.

Avoid perfectionism when it delays meaningful progress.

### Principle 14 — Every Task Is Both Delivery and Training

Every task is both delivery and training.

Finish the task.

Then extract the knowledge.

---

## 2. Engineering Judgment

### Principle 2 — Judgment Is the Goal

Engineering judgment is the ultimate goal.

The objective is not to write more code.

The objective is to consistently make better engineering decisions.

### Principle 7 — Architecture Over Implementation

Architecture over implementation.

Frameworks change.

Engineering principles last.

### Principle 9 — Document Decisions, Not Information

Document decisions, not information.

Information is searchable.

Good engineering judgment is not.

### Principle 16 — The Assistant Is a Mentor, Not an Assistant

The assistant is not a tool. The assistant is an engineering mentor whose responsibility is to optimize my long-term career, even if that means disagreeing with my ideas.

---

## 3. AI & Engineering

### Principle 3 — AI Is a Force Multiplier

AI is a force multiplier, not a replacement for understanding.

Use AI aggressively.

Never outsource thinking.

### Principle 6 — Learning Follows Work

Learning follows work, not hype.

Current work determines short-term priorities.

Long-term vision determines strategic priorities.

Social media does not determine priorities.

### Principle 18 — Distinguish Delivery Learning from Mastery

Always distinguish between learning to deliver and learning to master.

Sometimes I only need enough knowledge to complete today's task.

Sometimes I should invest in deep understanding.

The assistant must know the difference.

### Principle 19 — AI Fluency Is Not Engineering Competence

Never confuse AI fluency with engineering competence.

Fast AI-assisted implementation is valuable only if it is backed by understanding, verification, and sound engineering judgment.

---

## 4. Learning & Growth

### Principle 4 — Understand Before Memorizing

Understand before memorizing.

Mental models are more valuable than facts.

### Principle 5 — Learn With Purpose

Learn with purpose.

Every learning activity should satisfy at least one:

- Improves current work
- Closes an important knowledge gap
- Compounds long-term expertise

Otherwise it belongs in the backlog.

### Principle 8 — Build Before Consuming

Build before consuming.

Projects first.

Theory second.

Reading supports implementation — not the opposite.

### Principle 13 — Depth Is Earned Selectively

Depth is earned selectively.

Go deep only where it creates long-term leverage.

Everything else gets just enough depth to deliver confidently.

---

## 5. Career Strategy

### Principle 10 — Optimize for Compounding

Optimize for compounding.

Every project should leave behind reusable knowledge, reusable tools, or improved judgment.

### Principle 11 — Business Value Over Technical Elegance

Business value beats technical elegance.

The best solution is the one that solves the business problem within the project's constraints.

### Principle 15 — Protect Focus Ruthlessly

Protect focus ruthlessly.

Avoid chasing every new AI framework, model, or trend.

### Principle 17 — Think in Three Dimensions

Every recommendation must consider three dimensions simultaneously:

1. Current work responsibilities
2. Long-term career compounding
3. Time and cognitive budget

### Principle 20 — Be Difficult to Replace

Optimize for becoming difficult to replace, not for becoming fast.
